The Scientific Method

The scientific method is a special way that scientists learn about the world. It has 8 steps that help you discover new things! Click on each step to learn more.

What is it? Looking carefully at things and noticing what happens around you.

How to do it:

  • Watch things very carefully
  • Write down what you see
  • Use your senses - eyes, ears, nose, hands

Example: You notice that plants near the window grow better than plants in the corner.

What is it? Asking a question about what you noticed. Your question should be able to be tested!

How to do it:

  • Ask "why" or "how" questions
  • Make sure you can test your question
  • Ask one clear question

Example: "Do plants grow better with more sunlight?"

What is it? Learning what other people already know about your question.

How to do it:

  • Read books about your topic
  • Search the internet for information
  • Ask teachers or experts

Example: Reading about how plants use sunlight to grow and get energy.

What is it? A smart guess about what will happen based on what you already know.

How to do it:

  • Use what you learned to make a prediction
  • Write it as "If... then..."
  • Make sure you can test it

Example: "If plants get more sunlight, then they will grow bigger and taller."

What is it? Testing your prediction by doing a careful experiment.

How to do it:

  • Only change one thing (the thing you're testing)
  • Keep everything else the same
  • Write down everything that happens

Example: Put some plants in bright light and some in dim light. Give them the same water and soil. See which grows more.

What is it? Looking at all the information you collected and finding patterns.

How to do it:

  • Organize your measurements
  • Draw charts or graphs
  • Look for patterns in your results

Example: Making a graph that shows plant height vs. how much light they got. You see the pattern - more light = taller plants!

What is it? Deciding if your guess was right or wrong based on your results.

How to do it:

  • Compare your results to your prediction
  • Say if you were right or wrong
  • Explain what it means

Example: "My prediction was correct! Plants in bright light DID grow much bigger than plants in dim light."

What is it? Telling everyone what you discovered!

How to do it:

  • Write down your experiment and results
  • Present your project at school or science fairs
  • Share what you learned with others

Example: Making a poster board about your plant experiment and showing it at the school science fair!
